# My Final Project

## Project Name
Course Design Assistant


## Idea Selected
4. Course Design Assistant 


## Problem Statement
Designing a training course from scratch can take time, especially when
the trainer needs to organize learning objectives, topics, activities,
timing, and assessment.


## Target Users
Trainers, learning and development teams, course designers, and employees
who need to create training courses for non-technical audiences.


## R-C-T-F Prompt
You are an instructional design assistant specialized in creating
practical and easy-to-understand training courses.

The user will provide information about a training course, including:
- Topic
- Target audience
- Learner level
- Duration
- Delivery method
- Learning goal
- Topics to cover
- Course requirements

Your task is to design a structured training course based only on the
information provided.

Create:
1. A suitable course title.
2. Clear learning objectives.
3. A course structure with modules and estimated duration.
4. One practical learning activity.
5. A short assessment.
6. The expected learning outcome.

Do not invent organizational policies, facts, or requirements that were
not provided. If important information is missing, clearly identify it.

Format the response using clear headings and bullet points.

Present the course structure in a table with these columns:
- Module
- Topic
- Duration

Keep the language simple, practical, and suitable for the stated
target audience.

## Sample Output

### Sample Output (1):

# Course Design: Safe and Responsible Use of Generative AI at Work

## 1. Course Title

**Using Generative AI Safely and Responsibly at Work**

*A practical workshop for non-technical government employees*

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Explain in simple words what generative AI is and what it can and
  cannot do.
- Identify practical ways to use generative AI in their daily work.
- Recognize what information should not be shared with AI tools.
- Describe basic privacy and security risks when using AI tools.
- Check AI-generated content for errors before using it.
- Apply the basic principles of responsible AI use in their work.

## 3. Course Structure

**Format:** In-person  
**Learners:** 25  
**Total duration:** 2 hours (120 minutes)

| Module | Topic | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Welcome and introduction: course goals and a quick discussion of learners' current experience with AI | 10 min |
| 2 | What is generative AI? Simple explanation, examples of tools, strengths and limits | 15 min |
| 3 | Practical workplace use cases: drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, and improving writing | 15 min |
| 4 | What information should not be shared with AI tools: types of sensitive information and how to spot them | 15 min |
| 5 | Privacy and security: main risks and safe habits | 15 min |
| 6 | Checking AI-generated information: spotting errors, verifying facts, and reviewing before use | 15 min |
| 7 | Responsible use of AI: accountability, honesty, and good judgment | 10 min |
| 8 | Practical activity: "Safe or Not Safe?" | 15 min |
| 9 | Short assessment, wrap-up, and questions | 10 min |
| | **Total** | **120 min** |

## 4. Practical Learning Activity

**Activity name:** "Safe or Not Safe?" scenario workshop

**Duration:** 15 minutes

Learners are divided into 5 groups of 5.

Each group receives short workplace scenarios, for example:

- Asking an AI tool to make an email more polite.
- Pasting a full internal report into an AI tool to summarize it.
- Using an AI-written answer in a document without checking it.

For each scenario, the group decides:

1. Is this safe, unsafe, or safe with changes?
2. Why?
3. How could it be done more safely?

Each group shares one scenario with the room, and the instructor gives
short feedback.

**Materials needed:** Printed scenario cards, one set per group.

## 5. Short Assessment

A 5-question quiz:

1. In one sentence, what is generative AI?
2. Name two types of information you should not share with an AI tool.
3. True or false: If an AI tool gives a confident answer, it is always correct.
4. What is one thing you should do before using AI-generated content in
   official work?
5. Who is responsible for the final content you use: you or the AI tool?
   Why?

## 6. Expected Learning Outcome

After the course, employees will understand how to use generative AI
tools for simple daily tasks while protecting sensitive information,
checking AI outputs before use, and taking responsibility for the
content they produce.

## 7. Missing Information to Confirm

The assistant identified the following information that should be
confirmed before finalizing the course:

- Approved AI tools: Which tools, if any, are permitted in the organization?
- Organizational policy: Is there an existing AI or information security policy to reference?
- Definition of sensitive information: Should the organization's data classification be used?
- Language of delivery: Arabic, English, or bilingual?
- Live tool demo: Is internet access and a permitted AI tool available?
- Certificate or attendance record: Is one required?
- Instructor: Who will deliver the course, and do they need a facilitator guide?


### Sample Output (2):
# Course Design: Time Management and Prioritization at Work

## 1. Course Title

**Managing Your Time and Priorities at Work**

*A practical workshop for new government employees*

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Explain what a priority is and why prioritizing matters in their new role.
- Tell the difference between urgent and important tasks.
- Create a simple daily and weekly plan.
- Use simple ways to handle interruptions.
- Recognize common reasons for procrastination and use practical steps to start tasks.
- Review their own progress and adjust their plan.

## 3. Course Structure

**Format:** In-person  
**Learners:** 20  
**Total duration:** 90 minutes

| Module | Topic | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Welcome and introduction: course goals and a short discussion of learners' time challenges as new employees | 5 min |
| 2 | Understanding priorities: what a priority is and how to recognize the tasks that matter most | 10 min |
| 3 | Distinguishing urgent and important tasks: the four-quadrant urgent/important grid with simple workplace examples | 10 min |
| 4 | Daily and weekly planning: building a to-do list, choosing top tasks, and planning the week | 10 min |
| 5 | Managing interruptions: common interruptions and simple ways to respond | 10 min |
| 6 | Avoiding procrastination: common causes and practical tips to get started | 10 min |
| 7 | Reviewing progress: a short end-of-day and end-of-week review habit | 5 min |
| 8 | Practical activity: "Prioritize the Day" | 20 min |
| 9 | Short assessment, wrap-up, and questions | 10 min |
| | **Total** | **90 min** |

## 4. Practical Learning Activity

**Activity name:** "Prioritize the Day" scenario workshop

**Duration:** 20 minutes

Learners are divided into 4 groups of 5.

Each group receives a set of 8 to 10 task cards describing a typical
workday, for example:

- Reply to a colleague's routine email.
- Finish a report due tomorrow morning.
- Attend a scheduled team meeting.
- Tidy the shared files folder.
- Handle an unexpected request from a manager.

Each group places the cards on an urgent/important grid and chooses
their top three tasks for the day.

Each group presents its choices to the room, and the instructor gives
short feedback.

To finish, each learner writes their own top three tasks for tomorrow.

**Materials needed:** Printed task cards, flipchart paper and markers,
and sticky notes or blank paper.

## 5. Short Assessment

A 5-question quiz:

1. In one sentence, what is a priority?
2. Give one example of a task that is urgent but not important.
3. True or false: If a task is important, it must always be done first today.
4. Name one simple way to handle an interruption while working on a priority task.
5. Name one habit you will use to review your progress each day or week.

## 6. Expected Learning Outcome

After the course, new employees will be able to sort their tasks by
urgency and importance, plan their day and week, handle interruptions
and delays, and review their progress regularly to work more
effectively in their new role.

## 7. Missing Information to Confirm

The assistant identified the following information that should be
confirmed before finalizing the course:

- Organizational rules: Are there working hours, approval processes, or reporting rules that should be mentioned?
- Planning tools: Should learners use paper, a calendar, or a specific tool?
- Realistic examples: Should task cards reflect the organization's real tasks?
- Language of delivery: Arabic, English, or bilingual?
- Certificate or attendance record: Is one required?
- Instructor: Who will deliver the course, and is a facilitator guide needed?
  

## Safety Checklist
Before using the output, I checked that:

- The course content matches the information provided in the input.
- The assistant did not invent specific organizational policies.
- No confidential or personal information was included.
- The learning objectives are relevant to the stated course goal.
- The course duration is consistent with the duration provided in each input.
- Missing organizational information was clearly identified instead of being assumed.
